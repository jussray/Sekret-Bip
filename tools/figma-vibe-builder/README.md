## Visual Wonder / Attack 2000

Se’kret Bip inherits the portfolio Visual Wonder rule, but its expression is **family-first emotional worldbuilding**, not founder-tech proof graphics.

For every meaningful public still, moving hero, onboarding scene, product reveal, short clip, Reel/Short/TikTok asset, or cinematic product surface:

```text
human outcome
→ emotional intent
→ visual curiosity gap
→ world / scene / metaphor
→ product meaning
→ canon-safe motion
→ comprehension
→ Attack 2000
```

The goal is not merely “pretty.” The visual should make a child, teen, parent, or family member **want to look**, then understand the emotional/product meaning without being manipulated or buried in explanation.

### Required creative behavior

- Use wonder, warmth, revelation, belonging, safety, joy, tension, or awe deliberately rather than spraying effects everywhere.
- Treat explanatory UI as support, not the visual idea itself.
- Prefer a memorable scene, gesture, environmental beat, character reaction, light change, or motion reveal over a literal card that repeats the caption.
- Product truth must stay legible inside the beauty. Do not make a beautiful scene that lies about capability, privacy, safety, or availability.
- Motion must carry hierarchy, feeling, orientation, or cause-and-effect. Exporting static slides as MP4 does not become cinematic because the file moves.
- Preserve reduced-motion behavior and emotional safety.
- Preserve canon. Wonder may deepen the world; it may not change identity, face, costume, relationship, role, or family boundary without approved canon change.
- Never turn Se’kret Bip into generic “AI neon,” dashboard UI, or founder-control-room visual language.

### Attack 2000

`Attack 2000` is two falsification passes, not a claim that 2,000 external tests ran.

**Pass 1 — concept attack**

Attack the idea before implementation for:

- literalism;
- weak first-glance curiosity;
- emotional flatness;
- unclear audience world;
- canon drift;
- explanation replacing experience;
- motion that is decorative rather than meaningful;
- privacy/safety/product overclaim;
- generic AI-art language;
- failure to leave the human more oriented, curious, safe, or capable.

**Pass 2 — rendered artifact attack**

Attack the real implementation for:

- first-second/first-glance pull;
- beauty and wonder;
- comprehension;
- product meaning;
- character/family canon integrity;
- mobile containment and safe zones;
- text legibility;
- reduced motion;
- accessibility;
- clutter;
- animation timing;
- generic/AI-slop tells;
- emotional mismatch;
- whether the piece is memorable after the explanation disappears.

A truthful but visually dead experience is incomplete. A beautiful but confusing or misleading experience fails harder.

## Caveman visual grammar

The first interaction should be understandable before a visitor reads explanatory copy:

`◉ YOU → ☾ YOUR SPACE → ✦ ENTER`

Implementation rules:

1. The primer is temporary, visual-first, and pointer-safe.
2. The three beats reveal in order and disappear before normal interaction becomes the focus.
3. The entire arrival beat stays under one second when motion is allowed.
4. Reduced-motion visitors do not receive a forced animation.
5. The primer may not create horizontal overflow at the 320px phone boundary.
6. Motion teaches hierarchy. It does not decorate every object.
7. The primer is a teaching beat, not the permanent art direction. The family/world must regain visual ownership immediately afterward.

Bip Companion Style remains an original stylized cinematic 3D family-animation language: soft appealing shapes, expressive readable emotion, simplified believable anatomy, clean materials, gentle cinematic lighting, and emotionally safe warmth.

## /human output gate

Before approving a visual, answer:

- What should the child, teen, parent, or family understand after seeing this?
- What feeling helps them understand it?
- Does the visual preserve their dignity, choice, privacy, and emotional safety?
- Is there something worth looking at even before they read the text?
- Does the piece invite curiosity without tricking or pressuring them?
- Does the scene feel like Se’kret Bip, or could it belong to any generic AI product?

If the answer to the last question is “any AI product,” the visual is not done.
